1. Pillar Page:
   Generate 15–20 FAQs.

2. Support Page / Guide / Insight:
   Generate 8–10 FAQs.

3. Condition Page / Treatment Page:
   Generate 8–12 FAQs.

4. Clinical Trial Page:
   Generate 5–8 FAQs.

5. Single-question Answer Page:
   Generate 3–5 FAQs.

If the page type is not clearly specified, assume it is a Pillar Page and generate 18 FAQs.

FAQ ANSWER LENGTH:

Each FAQ answer should be approximately 65–75 words.

Do not write very long answers. Do not write thin one-line answers. Each answer should be long enough to be helpful but concise enough to be used by AI answer engines.

Each answer must begin with a direct, self-contained, quotable sentence. The first sentence should answer the question clearly without requiring the reader to see the rest of the page.

QUESTION STYLE:

Write questions exactly the way a patient or caregiver would search or ask a voice assistant.

Use natural question formats such as:

- What is [topic]?
- Who is eligible for [topic]?
- How does [topic] work?
- Is [topic] suitable for all cancer patients?
- What are the benefits of [topic]?
- What are the risks or side effects of [topic]?
- How much does [topic] cost?
- Is [topic] available in India?
- Which countries offer [topic]?
- How can international patients access [topic]?
- What reports are needed for [topic]?
- How does CancerFax help patients with [topic]?

Avoid overly academic or artificial questions. Do not use keyword-stuffed questions. Do not repeat the same question intent in different words.

Continue until the required number of FAQs is complete.

GROUPING RULES:

For Pillar Pages with 15–20 FAQs, group the FAQs under 3–5 plain-text H3-style headings.

Suggested groups:

- Understanding [Topic]
- Eligibility, Process, and Safety
- Cost, Access, and Countries
- Advanced Options and Clinical Trials
- Practical Questions for International Patients

For Support Pages, Guides, and Insights with 8–10 FAQs, use 2–3 groups only if helpful. If the topic is narrow, provide one flowing FAQ list without forced grouping.

ANSWER WRITING RULES:

Every answer must:

- Start with a direct answer in the first sentence.
- Be patient-friendly and easy to understand.
- Be medically cautious and balanced.
- Avoid promotional claims.
- Avoid exaggerated language.
- Avoid promising cure, guaranteed response, or assured treatment access.
- Use plain English.
- Avoid unnecessary medical jargon.
- Explain medical terms briefly when needed.
- Be understandable even if quoted alone by an AI engine.
- Include clinical context where relevant.
- Include caution language for treatment, eligibility, cost, trials, and international access.

MEDICAL SAFETY LANGUAGE:

Use safe phrases such as:

- “may be considered”
- “may be suitable for selected patients”
- “depends on cancer type, stage, biomarker profile, previous treatment, overall health, and specialist review”
- “should be discussed with a qualified oncology team”
- “final treatment decisions should be made by the treating medical team”

Never write:

- guaranteed cure
- 100% success
- best treatment for everyone
- no side effects
- risk-free
- assured results
- replaces standard cancer treatment
- CancerFax guarantees treatment or outcomes

COST FAQ RULES:

If including cost-related FAQs:

- Use estimated ranges only if reliable information is available.
- Mention that final cost depends on hospital, diagnosis, stage, tests, treatment protocol, complications, length of stay, and treating team advice.
- Do not present cost as fixed unless explicitly provided.
- Do not guarantee that a patient will be accepted at a hospital at a specific cost.

CLINICAL TRIAL FAQ RULES:

If including clinical trial FAQs:

- Do not guarantee enrollment.
- Do not guarantee benefit.
- Mention that eligibility is decided after medical record review and investigator assessment.
- Explain that trials may have strict inclusion and exclusion criteria.

INTERNATIONAL PATIENT FAQ RULES:

If the topic involves treatment abroad:

- Mention that access depends on hospital acceptance, medical review, visa approval, travel fitness, and patient condition.
- Do not guarantee visa approval, hospital admission, or treatment acceptance.
- Include practical questions about medical reports, second opinion, estimated cost, travel planning, and follow-up care where relevant.

CANCERFAX MENTION RULES:

Mention CancerFax only once or twice in the FAQ section.

Preferably include one final FAQ such as:
Q: How does CancerFax help patients with [topic]?

Use this type of answer:
A: CancerFax helps patients organize medical reports, identify relevant specialists or treatment centers, estimate possible costs, and coordinate second opinions, appointments, travel, visa, and follow-up support. Treatment decisions are always made by the qualified oncology team after reviewing the patient’s diagnosis, stage, biomarkers, previous treatment, and overall health.

Do not mention CancerFax in every answer. Do not make CancerFax sound like it replaces doctors or hospitals.

AEO OPTIMIZATION RULES:

Each FAQ should be optimized for answer engines by following these rules:

- The question must match real patient search intent.
- The first sentence must be a clear answer.
- The answer must be complete but concise.
- Avoid vague introductions like “It is important to understand that...”
- Avoid marketing phrases.
- Avoid duplicate intent.
- Use natural language.
- Include relevant entities such as treatment type, cancer type, country, test, biomarker, specialist, hospital, or clinical trial where appropriate.
- Make each answer useful even when separated from the page.

DO NOT INCLUDE:

- HTML
- Markdown tables unless specifically useful for a comparison question
- Raw JSON-LD schema unless specifically requested
- Excessive bullet points
- Repetitive questions
- Promotional claims
- Unverified survival statistics
- Guaranteed outcomes
- Claims that a treatment is suitable for everyone
- Overly technical explanations
- Long academic paragraphs
